\chapter{Introduction}\label{chapter:introduction}

The Zipfian distribution is a type of power-law distribution that appears in a wide range of natural and artificial phenomena,
including the frequency of words in natural language, city populations, and website traffic patterns \cite{mandelbrotNatureFractal1998}.
In recent years, it has also become highly relevant in the context of storage systems, databases, and network analysis, where operations
 such as read/write access or packet flows are often heavily skewed \cite{grayQuicklyGeneratingBillionrecord1994, crovellaPerformanceEvaluationHeavy2000}. 
 In such scenarios, a small subset of elements is accessed disproportionately often, while the majority remain rarely used.

 This skewed access pattern places specific demands on system design and performance testing. 
 To emulate real-world workloads, benchmark tools must generate samples that follow the Zipfian distribution—efficiently, 
 accurately, and at scale. This is especially important when the number of unique elements (i.e., the distribution's support size) is large. 
 Several tools are commonly used for this purpose, including YCSB, FIO, and Sysbench \cite{NavigatingDatabasePerformance2024}. However, the implementations of 
 Zipfian variate generation in these tools vary, and have not been comprehensively compared in terms of performance or statistical accuracy. 
 Deviations in accuracy could introduce biases significant enough to affect benchmark results \cite{TailScale}.

 This thesis addresses that gap. It provides a systematic review of Zipfian variate generation methods in widely-used benchmarking tools 
 and investigates alternative sampling algorithms drawn from the broader literature on discrete random variate generation. 
 The main contributions of this work are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item A review and comparison of Zipfian distribution implementations in common benchmarking tools.
  \item The implementation of alternative variate generation methods, drawn from outside the storage and systems community.
  \item A performance and accuracy evaluation of all methods, with recommendations based on empirical results.
\end{itemize}

By examining both existing tools and novel approaches, 
this work aims to provide guidance for future benchmarking practices and 
contribute to more reliable and representative performance evaluations.
